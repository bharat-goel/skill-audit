The fastest path once `release_02/` lands:

```
python3 scripts/run_all.py <release_root>
```

That runs inventory → text extraction → analytics → report in sequence and lands you at `release_root/REPORT.md`. Concretely:

1. Drop the new PDFs into `release_root/release_02/`.
2. Run `run_all.py <release_root>` — it's idempotent/incremental, so if extraction times out partway through (the war.gov FBI sections run 200+ pages), just re-run it and it picks up where it left off, or call `extract_text.py <release_root> <start> <end>` in chunks yourself.
3. Read `REPORT.md`.

Two things worth doing on a first pass at a *new* tranche rather than blindly trusting the pipeline:
- Check `inventory.csv`'s agency column for an `OTHER` bucket over ~3% of files — that means release 02 introduced filename prefixes not in `references/agency_vocab.md`, and you should add them there (and to `PREFIX_RULES` in `inventory.py`/`analyze.py`) before trusting the agency breakdown in the report.
- Since you'll have `release_01/` sitting alongside it, ask me for a "what's new vs release 01" comparison — that's a natural follow-up once both reports exist, but it isn't part of `run_all.py` itself.

Total time is dominated by text extraction on scanned pages with no text layer (those come back as 0-char `.txt` and get flagged as "OCR needed" rather than processed — OCR isn't run by default, by design, since Tesseract over thousands of scanned pages is hours, not minutes).
