In general, the fastest path for "raw dump → readable report" looks like:

1. **Ingest & OCR** (if scanned) — batch-extract text so everything is searchable/parseable.
2. **Triage/index** — a script that catalogs filenames, dates, page counts, and flags duplicates or empty scans, so you're not reading blind.
3. **Targeted extraction** — pull out whatever you actually care about (names, dates, redaction patterns, specific topics) rather than reading every page linearly.
4. **Draft synthesis** — have me (or a subagent) summarize per-document, then roll that up into one structured report.

The main tradeoff is thoroughness vs. speed: a keyword/topic-driven pass gets you a report fast but can miss things buried outside your search terms, while a full linear read-and-summarize is slower but safer for a first-time tranche. Given the scale of past DoD document releases, I'd lean toward the targeted pass first, then a follow-up full pass only on flagged documents.
